How I use Payhip to sell practical guides.

I did not need a warehouse, shipping department, or complicated ecommerce build. I needed a clean way to turn useful knowledge into something a customer could actually buy and receive.

Digital products · Direct services · Low overhead

The easiest way to overcomplicate a small digital business is to build the store before deciding what problem the product solves.

I went the other direction. Start with something useful. Package it clearly. Then use a platform that already knows how to handle the transaction and delivery.

Why Payhip fit the job.

For my practical technology material, I wanted low startup cost, no shipping, and no need to build my own payment and file-delivery system. Payhip gave me a simple storefront for digital products and a straightforward place to sell a direct service.

That let Ted Johnson Works stay focused on explanation, trust, and navigation while Payhip handles the transaction.

The product still has to earn its place.

A checkout page does not create value. The product has to solve a clear enough problem that somebody can understand why it exists.

For me, that means keeping the promise specific:

  • Everyday Tech Confidence: plain-English help for ordinary technology users.
  • Technology Clarity Call: thirty focused minutes for one confusing technology problem, message, purchase decision, or next step.

Keep the path short.

The customer should not have to decode the business model. They should be able to understand the problem, understand the offer, and click one obvious next step.

The model:

Useful knowledge → clear product → simple landing page → trusted checkout platform → delivery.

What I like about separating the website from checkout.

My main site does not have to become a store template. It can organize books, technology, AI, articles, and other work in a way that makes sense. When somebody is ready to buy a specific digital product or book a service, the button can take them to the platform built for that job.

That separation also makes the site easier to change. I can improve the content architecture without rebuilding a payment system every time.

What I would tell another creator.

Do not start by asking which platform has the most features. Start by asking what you can make that is genuinely useful and specific enough to explain in one sentence.

Then choose the lightest platform that can reliably deliver it.
