LEARN’N AS WE GO · EPISODE 1

Moving at the FREEZE of Business

Security should stop criminals—not stop business.

By Ted Johnson · Ted Johnson Works

After more than 30 years working in information technology, I thought I'd seen just about everything. Then I tried to change a phone number on a financial account.

What followed was an unexpected tour through authentication, old phone numbers, account recovery, backup codes, security checks, and waiting.

When I finally regained access, I discovered that updating the banking destination could be restricted for another two days.

Now, I understand the reason. If somebody steals an account, we absolutely do not want that person immediately redirecting the money. A waiting period on sensitive bank changes can protect the rightful owner.

But here's my question: Why does the legitimate customer so often feel like an unexpected problem rather than the person the system was built to protect?

We're changing how we prove who we are

Passwords and text messages are not the only options anymore. Passkeys use cryptographic credentials, sometimes unlocked with a device PIN, fingerprint, or face scan. A biometric unlock on a device is not the same thing as sending your biometric information to every website you visit.

According to the FIDO Alliance's 2026 global survey, 75% of the 11,000 surveyed consumers across ten countries had enabled passkeys on at least some accounts. That suggests a growing willingness to adopt easier authentication—not proof that everyone is using it successfully.

Meanwhile, IBM's 2026 Cost of a Data Breach study reported that one in four malicious breaches in its study were AI-enabled, a 56% increase over the prior year. Attackers can use AI to make impersonation more convincing. Defenders can also use automation to detect suspicious behavior.

Better locks need better ways back in

Changing your phone, losing a device, recovering after an injury, or forgetting a password should not make you a stranger to your own account. Recovery must be secure—but it also needs to be accessible and understandable.

NIST's digital identity guidance describes different levels of authentication assurance and ways to manage authenticators. There isn't a single perfect login method for every person or circumstance.

I don't want security protections removed. I want people to understand what is blocked, why it's blocked, how long it lasts, and what legitimate recovery options exist.

What should businesses measure?

We measure speed, uptime, automation, conversions, and efficiency. Shouldn't we also measure how long it takes an actual account owner to recover safe access?

Security can be technically strong while customer experience is painful. A fast system can also be dangerously insecure. Neither extreme is good enough.

Technology shouldn't leave humanity behind—and it shouldn't pass us by!

I'm still learning. That's why this series is called Learn’n as We Go. No pretending to have all the answers. Just experience, evidence, a sense of humor, and questions worth asking.

I'd like to hear your experience: Have you ever been locked out of your own account while trying to do something perfectly legitimate?
