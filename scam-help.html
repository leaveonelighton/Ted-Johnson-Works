Think It's a Scam?
Start Here.

One answer now. Not an apology later.

If money has moved, contact your bank or payment provider immediately. This page is a practical starting point, not a recovery service. No one can promise to retrieve stolen money.

1. Stop. Don't send more money.

Hang up, stop messaging, and don't click new links. Someone claiming to be a bank, the government, or “fraud support” might be impersonating them. Never transfer funds into a supposed “safe account.” Do not share one-time codes or allow unexpected remote access.

2. Call your real bank or payment company—NOW.

Use the phone number on the back of your card, official statement, or official app. Explain exactly how the money was sent. Ask whether the transaction can be stopped, recalled, disputed, or reversed. Contact the bank or service first; don't wait until you've filed a police report. Results depend on payment method and circumstances.

FTC: What to do if you were scammed

3. Secure accounts if information was exposed.

If you gave out a password or verification code, change the affected password from a trusted device, enable multi-factor authentication, and check other accounts where you reused the same password. If a scammer remotely accessed your computer, disconnect that access and seek trusted technical help before using it for banking.

FTC: Identity theft recovery steps

4. Report it using official websites.

For internet-enabled scams, report to the FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center. Report scams to the FTC. Reporting helps authorities identify patterns; it does not guarantee investigation or reimbursement.

FBI IC3FTC ReportFraud

5. Tell someone you trust. Save the evidence.

Ask a trusted friend or relative to help you think through the next steps. Preserve messages, transaction confirmations, dates, phone numbers, and names used. Never pay an upfront fee to someone promising to recover lost money. Recovery scams often target people who've already been harmed.

Still on the phone with someone demanding action?

Hang up. Contact the institution yourself using a trusted number. Someone knowing your name or account details does not prove they work there. Never act on a request to keep the matter secret.
